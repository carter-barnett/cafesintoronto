Summer in Toronto is short, and the city's best outdoor cafe spaces fill up fast. If you've already read [our patio guide from May](/blog/best-cafe-patios-in-toronto), this is the follow-up: eight more cafes with confirmed outdoor seating, spread across the city, with the hours and details you need to actually show up and get a seat.

These are all different from the ones we covered in spring. New set, same standard.

---

## [Berkeley Cafe](/cafes/berkeley-cafe/) — Downtown Toronto

![Berkeley Cafe exterior and outdoor seating area, Downtown Toronto](/photos/berkeley-cafe.jpg)

Berkeley Cafe is the kind of place that earns a 4.9-star rating without trying to. It sits in Downtown Toronto and has built a following of regulars who come for the coffee and stay because there's nowhere else they'd rather be. The outdoor seating is part of that draw — open, comfortable, and without the performance of some of the splashier spots nearby.

Open Monday through Friday from 7:30am to 6pm, with Saturday hours from 10am to 5pm (closed Sunday). That weekday window is where Berkeley shines: the morning crowd is orderly, the coffee is consistently well-made, and the outdoor seat you secure before 9am will serve you well for an hour or two.

This is not a flashy terrace. It is a very good one.

**Open:** Mon–Fri 7:30am–6pm, Sat 10am–5pm

## [Thindi Cafe](/cafes/thindi-cafe/) — Liberty Village

![Thindi Cafe outdoor seating in Liberty Village, Toronto](/photos/thindi-cafe.jpg)

Thindi is one of the most interesting cafes in [Liberty Village](/blog/liberty-village-cafe-guide) and one of the few in the city doing something genuinely different. The South Asian-inspired menu — think cardamom lattes, masala chai, and flavours you won't find at the standard Toronto specialty shop — gives it a distinct identity that holds up in both the food and the drinks.

What makes Thindi stand out for summer specifically is the hours: open every day from 10am to 9pm. That's a late close for a cafe, and it means the outdoor seating remains a realistic option well into the evening — not just a morning window before it gets too crowded or too hot. A weekday evening here, with something from the specialty drinks menu and the air starting to cool off, is one of the better summer cafe experiences in the city.

Rated 4.8★ across 188 reviews. Worth the walk from King Street.

**Open:** Daily 10am–9pm

## [Project Seoul](/cafes/project-seoul/) — Chinatown

Project Seoul is a Chinatown cafe with Korean-inspired touches — in the drinks, the aesthetic, and the way the space is put together. It has 1,044 Google reviews at 4.7★, which for a cafe this distinctive is an unusual combination: typically a place this specific in its vision divides people, but Project Seoul seems to have landed on something that works broadly.

The outdoor seating matters most here in summer because the cafe stays open until 10pm every day of the week. If you want to sit outside in the evening — actually in the evening, not just until 6pm — Project Seoul is one of the few cafes in the city where that is reliably possible. The Chinatown location also means you're in a neighbourhood with things happening around you, which makes for good ambient energy.

**Open:** Daily 11am–10pm

## [Nonni Cafe & Bakery](/cafes/nonni-cafe-bakery/) — King West

![Nonni Cafe & Bakery outdoor seating on King West, Toronto](/photos/nonni-cafe-bakery.jpg)

[King West](/blog/king-west-cafe-guide) has no shortage of cafes, but Nonni holds its own with an Italian-leaning identity that shows in both the pastry case and the coffee program. The croissants are the real draw — the kind that flake properly, without the airy disappointment of a cafe treating baked goods as an afterthought.

The outdoor seating here benefits from long hours: Nonni is open 7:30am to 8pm every day of the week, which is uncommon. Most cafes that have a proper kitchen and decent baked goods are closed by 5pm. Nonni staying open until 8pm means the terrace is an option for an afternoon coffee that turns into early evening, without having to track down somewhere else before the sun goes down.

Rated 4.7★ across 397 reviews. Consistent enough that the reviews are almost boring in their agreement.

**Open:** Daily 7:30am–8pm

## [Found Coffee | Bellwoods](/cafes/found-coffee-bellwoods/) — Liberty Village

Found Coffee sits on the edge of Liberty Village, close enough to Trinity Bellwoods Park that it functions as a natural stop before or after time in the park. This is its best use in summer: you're going to the park, you want good coffee, and Found Coffee is there. The outdoor seating continues that logic — it's a cafe that seems to understand its neighbourhood and serve it accordingly.

Rated 4.7★ across 478 reviews. Open daily from 8am to 4pm, which means morning and early afternoon are the windows. Don't plan on an evening visit here. But if you want to make the most of a summer morning before the park fills up, this is a strong choice.

The coffee is well-made and the space is easy to be in. Sometimes that's exactly what you need.

**Open:** Daily 8am–4pm

## [FIKA Cafe](/cafes/fika-cafe/) — Kensington Market

![FIKA Cafe outdoor seating in Kensington Market, Toronto](/photos/fika-cafe.jpg)

Fika is named after the Swedish tradition of a proper coffee break — a deliberate pause rather than a coffee on the move. In Kensington Market in summer, that concept translates well: the neighbourhood is good for slow mornings, and Fika's outdoor seating is good for taking them.

With 1,415 Google reviews at 4.6★, Fika is one of the more reviewed independent cafes in [Kensington Market](/blog/kensington-market-cafe-guide). The volume of reviews says something about how consistently it meets expectations. The outdoor seating fills up quickly on weekends, so weekday mornings are the better bet.

Hours open slightly later than most: 10:30am on most weekdays, 10am on Fridays and Saturdays. This is not a 7am-and-out place. It's a mid-morning cafe for people who are not in a hurry, and the summer outdoor experience reflects that pace.

**Open:** Mon–Thu 10:30am–5pm, Fri 10am–5pm, Sat 10am–6pm, Sun 10:30am–5pm

## [Arvo Coffee](/cafes/arvo-coffee/) — Distillery District

The [Distillery District](/blog/distillery-district-cafe-guide) in summer attracts a lot of traffic, and Arvo handles that well. Australian-influenced in its coffee approach — flat whites, careful espresso, nothing fussy — Arvo has one of the better patios in the district. That's meaningful given the competition from Balzac's nearby.

Where Arvo distinguishes itself is in the coffee program itself: the Australian cafe influence tends to produce espresso that's more fruit-forward and less roast-heavy, which pairs better with summer drinking than dark roasts do. The outdoor seating faces into the district, which means you get the cobblestone atmosphere without being entirely inside it.

Rated 4.5★. Open daily with hours that align with the district's foot traffic. If you're spending time in the Distillery this summer and want specialty coffee with a genuine outdoor seat, Arvo is the move.

## [Coffee Oysters Champagne](/cafes/coffee-oysters-champagne/) — King West

Coffee Oysters Champagne does the unexpected combination its name promises. On King West, where cafes compete hard for identity, COC has found one that's genuinely distinctive: coffee in the morning, oysters and champagne as the day moves on, and a space that works for both. The outdoor seating fits the same register — a bit more considered than most, without being uncomfortable about it.

Rated 4.5★. Tagged as both aesthetic and great for dates, which is an accurate description of what the outdoor space offers. Summer evenings here have an energy that a lot of cafes fail to create: relaxed but not dead, social but not loud.

This is also a practical pick if you want a summer destination rather than just a coffee stop. The menu evolves across the day in a way that makes it useful for longer stays. You can arrive for coffee and leave after something that felt more like an event.

## A few notes on using this list

Toronto's patio season runs roughly May through September, with July and August being the reliable window where you can count on outdoor seating being pleasant. Most of the cafes above are open across that window, but hours can change — especially for weekend versus weekday service.

A couple of patterns worth knowing before you go:

**Evening outdoor seating is rare.** Most Toronto cafes close between 5pm and 6pm, which limits the outdoor season to mornings and early afternoons. Project Seoul (open until 10pm) and Thindi Cafe (open until 9pm) are the standout exceptions on this list if you want outdoor coffee after work.

**Weekday mornings are better than weekend mornings.** The popular outdoor spaces — particularly Fika in Kensington and Found Coffee near Bellwoods — fill up quickly on Saturday and Sunday. If you have flexibility, Tuesday at 10am is an easier outdoor seat than Saturday at 11am.

**The Distillery District gets busy.** Arvo is worth seeking out, but the Distillery attracts significant foot traffic in summer. Going on a weekday, or early on a weekend morning before the market crowd arrives, makes the whole experience better.

For more on Toronto's outdoor cafe options, the [patio guide published in May](/blog/best-cafe-patios-in-toronto) covers eight additional spots with confirmed outdoor seating — including Fungo, Balzac's Distillery, and Jimmy's on Ossington. Between the two lists, you have a solid starting point for the rest of the summer.
